# COFIRE demo film: script

> **DRAFT FOR THE STUDENT.** The narration below was drafted by an AI agent
> as a starting point. It is meant to be rewritten in your own words and
> recorded in your own voice (see `RECORDING_GUIDE.md`). Keep the facts.
> Change everything else. When you change a line, edit
> `script/narration.json`. The captions, the scratch voice and the storyboard
> are all built from that file.

Target length is 2:30 to 2:55 (the render fails over 3:00). Five parts:
problem, product, the moment it clicks, why to trust it, where it goes. The
product is on screen for most of the film, and there are no slides.

## Rules this script follows

- **Numbers come from data.** Every number in the narration and on screen
  comes from `app/lib/facts.json` or a project artifact, inserted at render
  time. In `script/narration.json` a number is a field reference such as
  `{n_survivors}`, never a typed digit, and the render fails on any number it
  cannot trace. The table below shows each line with its fields filled in
  from today's data.
- **Outside facts have a source.** Each one gets an on-screen source line
  and an entry in `SOURCES.md`. There are only two, both from Denver Water
  pages the app already cites.
- **Claims stay inside what the app supports:** calibrated odds, the
  survivors, the sealed-year protocol and the retraction. The film never
  says it "predicts fires" or makes "accurate forecasts". The fire targets
  are conditions that let fire spread, not ignitions.
- **Corrections to the brief, made here so the film stays true:**
  - The survivor count is 22 of 115 (`multiplicity.n_survivors`), not
    eleven. Eleven was an older count from two areas.
  - 2026 has been looked at three times, not once (`hindcast_looks`). The
    film says every look is counted, and that scoring the full year opens on
    the unseal date.
  - There is no address search in the app. The magic moment picks Denver
    from the area picker and taps a square.

## The arc

| Part | Time | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Cold open | 0:00 to 0:10 | The 2026 drought, week by week, from the data the app published. One line of kinetic type. No logo yet. |
| 2. The problem | 0:10 to 0:30 | Denver feels far from drought and fire, but about half its water comes from mountain watersheds that can burn. The map pulls back from Denver to the two source watersheds, and the Roberts Tunnel is traced from Dillon Reservoir. |
| 3. The product | 0:30 to 1:20 | Pick Denver, the map flies in, tap a square, the Place Card resolves. The frequency sentence, the icon array, "weeks like this one", the watershed panel, the Spanish toggle and the printable brief. All real captures. |
| 4. Why trust it | 1:20 to 2:10 | The frozen model and counted looks, then 22 of 115 surviving a shuffled control. Then the turn: the ranges that looked perfect, the leak found in the project's own test, the collapse, and the ranges removed with the reason on the card. This is the emotional peak, so pace it as one. |
| 5. Impact and close | 2:10 to 2:50 | The weekly refresh, the timestamped and fingerprinted record, scoring at the unseal, and the open data. End on the live map, the app name and the URL. |

## Kinetic type (three or four lines only)

1. Cold open: **"2026, week by week."** (year from data)
2. Part 4 open: **"Why trust it?"**
3. The turn: **"It looked perfect."**
4. End card: **COFIRE**, the tagline, and the URL.

## Notes for recording

- Read it like you'd explain it to a friend, not like an ad. Short
  sentences. Leave a breath after line 17 ("It looked perfect.") and before
  line 18. The silence is the turn.
- The film is cut to the line start times above. If your reading runs
  longer, keep each line inside its slot (the guide's prompter video shows
  the slots), or retime with whisper.cpp as `RECORDING_GUIDE.md` explains.
- Say "COFIRE" the same way every time.
